MODULE 4.4

📐 Shot Sizes & Camera Movement

The two most essential tools of visual storytelling: shot size (from establishing shot to ECU) and camera movement (25+ of them). You'll learn not just their names, but why directors choose them and how each one shapes emotion.

1

📏 Shot Size Is an Emotional Decision

Shot size isn't just about camera distance. It's a emotional and narrative decision.

Wide shots

Respond: where are we?

Close-ups

Respond: how does it feel?

Extreme close-ups

Reveal details that can change the story.

👉 Golden rule

Start open to establish the world. Move closer to reveal emotion. End on a close-up or extreme close-up for maximum impact.

2

🪜 From Establishing Shot to ECU

The shot scale, from the widest to the tightest. Each step has a specific narrative purpose.

ShotShows
Establishing / Establishing shotLocation, weather, and scale
Wide / Wide ShotIsolation, danger, or power
Medium Wide / CowboyAction and body language
Medium / Medium ShotNeutral, realistic dialogue or movement
Medium Close-UpEmotional reaction and intimacy
Close-UpStrong emotion — joy, fear, or determination
Extreme Close-UpDetails, suspense, and narrative clues

👉 Simple decision rule

  • •If the location or action matters more → wide shot.
  • •If the emotion or reaction matters more → close-up.
  • •If a a detail changes the story → extreme close-up.
3

🎞️ Dolly vs. Zoom vs. Dolly Zoom

The critical distinction in all camera language is between camera movement e lens movement.

Dolly

The camera physically moves through space → parallax, depth, and realism. Immersion.

Zoom

The lens changes the focal length without moving the camera → flatter and more observational. Documentary style.

Dolly Zoom

Movement and zoom oppose each other → spatial distortion. Disorientation and tension.

4

🧭 Truck, Pan, Tilt, Crane

The coverage moves that organize the scene’s space: move laterally, sweep, and reveal.

➡️ Truck

The camera moves sideways to reveal the surroundings from the side.

↔️ Pan

Horizontal pan from a fixed axis.

↕️ Tilt

Vertical pan. Tilt up reveals from bottom to top.

🏗️ Crane

Broad vertical spatial movement → emphasizes scale and grandeur.

5

🌐 Orbit, Arc, Whip Pan, Rack Focus

The moves that give the shot a cinematic finish — premium, dynamic, and connective.

🔄 Orbit / Arc

The camera circles the subject → depth and a premium cinematic feel.

💨 Whip Pan

Ultra-fast pan → dynamic transition and energy.

🎯 Rack Focus

Shifts focus from one shot to another → connects visual elements.

⚡ Crash Zoom / Fast Dolly In

Sudden push-in → impact and shock.

Parallax: orbit and arc separate the foreground, subject, and background, increasing depth—which gives AI-generated videos a premium cinematic look.

6

🤳 Handheld & Dutch Angle

Two direct shortcuts to visceral impact and psychological tension.

🤳 Handheld

  • →Realism and documentary energy
  • →Raw immersion, physical presence

📐 Dutch Angle

  • →Dutch angle → discomfort
  • →Instability and psychological tension
7

🎯 Practical Guide: Intention → Movement

The goal isn’t to know what to use, but when and why. Focus on intentionality — not just how the shot looks, but exactly why that movement was chosen.

To create...Use
Emotional closeness / intimacydolly in
Scale or loneliness / isolationdolly out
Reveal vertically, from bottom to toptilt up
Connect visual elementsrack focus
Premium cinematic feelarc shot
Realism or documentary energyhandheld
Discomfort or instabilitydutch angle
Impact or shockfast dolly in / crash zoom
Reveal the environment from the sidetruck movement
Dynamic transitionwhip pan
Distort perception and induce tensiondolly zoom

🎬 The final rule

The more needs to the better your camera language, the more cinematic the result will be. Every movement is an emotional choice — not an effect.

📝 Task — Intentional Shots and Camera Moves

Combine the module’s two topics in short exercises:

  • 📏Shot: create a 5-second cinematic prompt — choose a shot size and explain why a director would use it to evoke a specific emotion. Include format, setting, lighting, character, and two timestamps with action (practical effects, natural light, no CGI).
  • 🎥Movement: create 3 short videos with different movements. For each one, write the prompt and explain why you chose that movement and what emotional purpose it serves.
  • 🎯Objective: intentionality — not just how the shot looks, but exactly why that shot and movement.
